# Knot 1.1.127

**Everyone in a call or share needs 1.1.127.** Calls and screen shares changed
how they travel. A friend on an older Knot cannot join a new call or watch a new
share; Knot tells them so and asks them to update.

## Screen sharing, rebuilt

- **Nothing is lowered for the connection, ever.** A share keeps the codec, size,
  frame rate and bitrate its sender picked. If a viewer's connection cannot keep
  up, that viewer waits behind the buffering cats with the last picture on
  screen, then catches up. Every picture the sharer captured is delivered, whole
  and in order, instead of being dropped to fit the link.
- **Pictures go straight to your friend over a fast, encrypted UDP connection**
  when both networks allow it, and over the encrypted direct connection
  otherwise. In our tests a share showed its first picture in about a second
  and ran roughly a third of a second behind live; how it goes on your
  connection depends on the connection.
- **The picture quality is decided by what the sharer's computer can encode, not
  by the network.** On Linux with an NVIDIA or AMD GPU, the GPU encodes AV1 for
  the whole share. On Windows and everywhere else, Knot encodes AV1, H.264 or
  VP9 itself, whichever your computer can really keep up with at the size and
  rate you picked (it measures this when you press Go Live, in about a second).
  If your computer cannot keep up, the share says so.
- **Desktop mode sharpens text.** Choosing **Desktop** in the share dialog turns
  on AV1's screen-content tools: in a test, text came out at half the bitrate
  and visibly cleaner. **Game** keeps smooth motion. The VP8 choice is gone.
- **Group shares send only to people who are watching.** Click a friend's live
  badge to watch; nothing is sent to someone who has not.
- **Sound stays in step with the picture.** Share sound is held back by exactly
  as much as the picture, so lips and clicks line up.
- **The line under a friend's share is back** and now shows the picture size,
  frames per second arriving, bitrate, codec, and whether your GPU or your CPU
  decodes it. If frames arrive but are not shown, it says this computer is
  falling behind; if nothing arrives, it says your friend's connection.
- **A GPU decoder that shows black can no longer blank a share.** The first
  picture a hardware decoder produces is compared with a software decode of the
  same picture. If they disagree, Knot switches to the software decoder at once.
- The Linux screen recorder is stopped when you stop sharing, and can no longer
  outlive Knot.

## Calls that connect faster

- **Joining a call is one click, and calls set themselves up while they ring.**
  There is no refreshing the chat, and both of you pressing Call at the same
  moment now ends up in the same call.
- **A call asks the server for less.** Setup uses a handful of small messages
  through the relay Knot already uses, and nothing about a call is stored. The
  relay is only asked for after a direct connection has not come up within a few
  seconds.

## Files

- **Fast direct transfer (UDP).** Files use a fast UDP connection to your friend
  when both networks allow it, and the encrypted direct connection otherwise. On
  a long or lossy connection it can be many times faster than before. Nothing is
  uploaded to a server. **Settings → File transfers → Fast direct transfer (UDP)**
  turns it off.
