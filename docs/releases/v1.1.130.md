# Knot 1.1.130

Screen shares and calls from 1.1.129 still work with this version in both
directions; nothing about how they travel changed.

This release comes from going through everything the screen share does and
measuring it, instead of guessing: a link lab that shapes the network (delay,
loss, jitter, reordering, speed limits), a randomised stress test of the viewer,
and the real apps start and stop shares dozens of times. What it found is below.

## A slow link no longer leaves you watching the past

- **A share on a link that cannot carry it stays near live.** The sharer kept up
  to 45 seconds of pictures for a viewer that was falling behind. On a link
  slower than the stream, the picture got half a second older every second
  (measured: 19 seconds old after 40 seconds, and still climbing), and nothing
  on the viewer's side could skip pictures that had not arrived. The limit is
  now 6 seconds: a viewer that falls that far behind is moved up to the latest
  key picture, and the picture itself is never made worse.
- **You are told when this is happening.** The person sharing sees "your friend
  is 7.4 s behind: the connection cannot carry this bitrate", and whether that
  friend is on the slow route; the viewer sees "playing 2.5 s behind live".
- **A viewer on the slow route is told so.** When the fast UDP connection cannot
  be made, a share rides the browser's data channel, which measured badly: it
  takes seconds to reach 40 Mbit/s at 40 ms round trip, stops near 10 Mbit/s at
  100 ms, and half a percent of packet loss leaves it near 3 Mbit/s. The viewer's
  line now says "on the slower data-channel route" after 12 seconds without UDP.

- **A viewer that keeps falling behind now tells the sharer what it really
  receives.** That report existed before the share engine was rebuilt and had
  been dead since: nothing set it, so the sharer sized a share from speed tests
  alone, which measure the two computers' connections and not the path between
  them. Now, when the sender has had to move a viewer up for being 6 seconds
  behind, or the picture has sat 1.5 seconds behind live for 5 seconds, the
  viewer announces the median rate it was receiving, and stops 20 calm seconds
  after it has recovered. The next share the sharer starts is sized to it (a
  slider set to 200 Mbps is held to 92% of what the friend receives), and the
  sharer's line says it ("they are receiving about 14 Mbps; the next share you
  start is sized to that"). Nothing is lowered during a share.

## The speed test

- **The speed test now works.** Its upload half never produced a result: it
  opened its measuring window only after a write that reported room, but each
  256 KB chunk is bigger than a stream's buffer, so every write reported "full"
  and the window never opened. The released code gave up after 17 seconds with
  nothing on a 100 Mbit/s link (the fixed one measures 91 down on it), so Knot
  never had a measure of your connection: no ceiling on the bitrate slider, and
  none on what a share is sized to. Upload still reads high (it counts bytes
  handed to the connection, not bytes delivered), which the existing 25% derating
  absorbs.
- **It no longer hammers the test server.** `speed.cloudflare.com` answers
  "429, retry in 54 minutes" to a computer that asks too often, and Knot asked
  at every launch and again whenever a share was stopped with no result kept. A
  refused test is now left alone for as long as the server asked (up to an hour),
  one that fails for another reason for two minutes, and test copies of Knot never
  run it at all.
- **The bitrate slider no longer rewrites what you chose.** It used to save the
  measured limit over your setting, so with a working speed test one low reading
  (something else using your connection at the time) would have replaced a saved
  200 Mbps for good. The slider now shows the limit ("30 Mbps (you chose 200)")
  and your choice stays; a share is held to the same limit when it starts.

## Viewer

- **A rough patch no longer leaves you behind live for minutes.** Each time the
  picture ran dry the viewer waited longer behind the newest picture (and the
  sound was held back with it), and it gave that time back at 20 ms a second.
  After 40 seconds of overload that meant 4.4 seconds behind live, and four
  minutes (232 s, measured with the real scheduler) of a perfect link before it
  was back to normal. It now gives it back at 10% of the delay a second, but only
  while the buffer has not been used (it stayed above 70% of the delay for the
  last 10 to 20 seconds): 51 seconds to recover, and a link that keeps breaking up
  keeps its delay and stalls less often than before (0.8 to 1.5 times a minute in
  the 10-minute test, it was 1.7 to 2.2).

- **Pictures waiting to be shown were never freed on a skip, a resize or a
  restart from a key picture.** Each one is a 12 MB picture at 4K (or one of the
  few slots of a hardware decoder). Found by the new randomised player test (400
  sessions with a decoder that is late, holds pictures back, fails and ignores
  flushes), and fixed.
- **One failed picture no longer ends the share.** A software decoder that failed
  once left "this computer cannot show the stream" on the screen and repeated it
  for every following picture. It now starts a new decoder and waits for the next
  key picture (up to four times in 30 seconds), and an error is reported once.
- **"On GPU" is now only said when it is true, in Settings as well as under the
  share.** Chromium answers "yes" to a hardware request it then serves on the CPU
  (an RTX 4090 under Linux "decoded AV1 in hardware" with every picture made by
  the CPU decoder). Knot now decodes a short test clip for AV1, H.264 and VP9
  with a hardware preference and counts a codec only if the pictures come out as
  a GPU makes them (NV12) and not planar (I420); the line under a share goes by
  the pictures themselves.
- **A hardware decoder that fails on a real stream is taken off the list.** One
  that passed the test clip and then had to be given up (it refused the stream,
  produced nothing, or showed a different picture than the CPU) used to stay
  listed in Settings and in what friends are told.
- **The line says why fewer pictures are shown than arrive**: the window is
  redrawn only so many times a second, or this computer decodes only so many a
  second, instead of always "falling behind".
- **A GPU decoder check that failed once is asked again.** If the check for
  Knot's own NVIDIA decoder timed out at launch (easy while a game is using the
  machine), the viewer stayed on the CPU decoder until Knot was restarted. A
  "no" that may pass is now asked again every 30 seconds; a final "no" (this GPU
  cannot decode AV1) is not.

## Sound

- A share with no sound at all used to be reported as "sound arriving but
  silent". The viewer now says "your friend is not sharing sound" or, once sound
  was shared, "your friend's sound is silent" after six quiet seconds.
- When sound capture cannot start the person sharing is told why (it stayed on
  "starting sound capture"), and capture that hears nothing says so ("sound on,
  but nothing is playing on this computer"). On Linux it never left "waiting for
  computer sound"; it now says live as soon as sound plays.

## The share log

While a share is on, Knot writes one line a second to `share-diagnostics.log` in
its own folder (`~/.config/Knot/` on Linux, `%APPDATA%\Knot\` on Windows): what
arrives and what is shown, delay and stalls, what decodes it and the format of
its pictures, which lane carries it, how long the page was blocked, the sound
line, how far behind each viewer is, how busy each Knot process is, and the UDP
connection's own numbers (window, round trip, packets sent again, recoveries,
the bandwidth it measures). It holds numbers and the names of codecs and
decoders only, about the last hour or two are kept, it can be read only by you,
and it is never sent anywhere. Group shares are included.

## Smaller fixes

- The recorder's video stream parser read nothing from a cluster of unknown
  size written in the eight-byte form FFmpeg uses when it streams live (the
  recorder's own output does not use it, so this was not hit).
- Node's "11 drain listeners" warnings from the UDP connection are gone.

## Behind the scenes

- New tests: the viewer under random abuse; the WebM parser under random
  chunking, 3 MB pictures, embedded cluster ids, corruption and a real FFmpeg
  stream; the hardware decode list; the share log and the lines the page writes;
  lag, route and decoder-retry behaviour; and a 1080p58 H.264 share through the
  real player.
- The test runner now stops a run that does not finish in four minutes and tries
  it once more (an Electron start-up stall once held a whole suite for 17
  minutes), and the end-to-end rig can keep the app's own graphics settings
  (`E2E_GPU=1`).
