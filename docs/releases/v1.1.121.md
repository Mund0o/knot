# Knot 1.1.121

## Screen share

- **Shares no longer fall seconds behind on viewers that decode on the CPU.**
  Since 1.1.118, NVIDIA Linux decodes received video in software. Sharers still
  raised their bitrate toward the GPU ceiling (up to 200 Mbps on a LAN), which a
  CPU decoder cannot keep up with, so the picture lagged further and further
  behind. Viewers now tell the sharer which codecs their GPU decodes, and a
  sharer keeps CPU-decoding viewers on the normal bitrate curve (about 16 Mbps
  at 4K60). GPU-decoding viewers are unchanged.
- **Windows shares to Linux keep their hardware encoder.** A Linux viewer used
  to force VP9, which most Windows GPUs cannot encode, so the share was encoded
  on the CPU. With the codec setting on Automatic, the sharer's own choice is
  kept. An explicit codec choice in Settings still applies.
- Share viewers now notice packet loss and freezes when judging whether the
  connection is congested. Both were always counted as zero before.

## NVIDIA GPU video decoding on Linux

- **NVIDIA Linux decodes shared screens on the GPU again** when
  nvidia-vaapi-driver 0.0.18 or newer is installed. That release exports
  decoded pictures in the layout Chromium can draw, which fixes the white video
  that made 1.1.118 fall back to the CPU.
- Knot checks this itself at every launch: it decodes a small four-colour AV1
  picture on the GPU and confirms the colours. If the check fails, Knot records
  it for that driver build and restarts once on CPU decoding, so an older or
  broken driver can never bring the white screen back. Updating the driver
  runs the check again.
- **Settings → Screen sharing** now says whether shared screens you watch
  decode on your GPU or your CPU.
